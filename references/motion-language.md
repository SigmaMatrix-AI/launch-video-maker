# Motion language: composition, time, and attention

Use these principles in stage three to express the confirmed story. Record only consequential choices in the storyboard; no fixed layout, effect list, or timing ratio is required. Use [E01–E10](eval.md) to identify observable issues.

## Compose for the current idea

The whole canvas is available: full-frame imagery, cropping, close-ups, edge entrances and exits, and off-center compositions can all direct attention. Choose scale and position from what the viewer needs to notice. Negative space should support meaning, contrast, or anticipation. Text safe areas protect essential copy; they do not require every visual to fit inside a centered box or share uniform padding. Centered compositions, generous margins, and full-bleed shots are choices, not defaults every shot must follow.

## Allocate time by importance

Compress secondary operations, show the cause and result of key actions, and allow reading once information is clear. Vary emphasis across the film. Fast movement, steady scans, hard cuts, and still holds each have uses; judge whether the viewer can follow the change. Choose acceleration and settling for the object's role rather than adding one easing or spring everywhere. With sound, let phrases and accents support the reveals without forcing a cut on every beat.

## Let text express meaning

Choose the message, emphasis words, and semantic groups before animating. Preserve meaningful phrases, brand terms, and numbers with units. Give text room to lead a shot or connect it to the product through a clear attention handoff; keep necessary labels in real UI.

| Intention | Decision and check |
| --- | --- |
| Build an idea in stages | Reveal meaningful groups; allow each to register without stretching a paragraph into a character-by-character wait. |
| Compare alternatives | Keep shared words stable and replace only the changing term; size for the longest word and check overlap and reading time. |
| Land on a key point | Use contrast, scale, position, or focus to reach a clear endpoint; settled text stays sharp and readable. |
| Carry an idea into the product | Track the same object into a real input or result when the product supports that relationship. |

## Judge the actual sequence

Use the [real motion sequences](eval.md#motion-case-cards) to study state changes and object relationships. The animated excerpts retain playback speed at 24 fps and contain no audio; use the original videos for frame-accurate timing, easing, and audiovisual rhythm. Follow the [technical conventions](tech.md) for frame determinism and asset readiness.

Watch the current film at normal speed: where does attention go, can the message be read, and does the change communicate something? Inspect intermediate frames for overlap, clipping, and seams. Revise the affected segment and both transitions, then rewatch the full film and recheck sound after timing changes. Record evidence using the [review protocol](tech.md#quality-assurance); technical passes and stills alone do not establish visual quality.
