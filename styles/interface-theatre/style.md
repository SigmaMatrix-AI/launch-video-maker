# Lavender Interface Theatre

A motion language for explaining digital tools through luminous, floating interfaces. Pale lavender collages make a process feel dispersed; near-black stages isolate a decisive action. Restrained violet connects the two worlds. The identity comes from this light/dark contrast, concrete product behaviour, and theatrical changes of scale—not from a particular product, logo, or task.

This extraction is based on one English-language product film. Repeated features below are observed core grammar; numerical recipes and font matching are inferred defaults. Scene counts, exact camera paths and copy remain variable. The film supports a family of compositions, not a universal storyboard.

## Two related stages

**Core, observed:** Alternate an airy, near-white lavender stage with a near-black indigo stage, keeping violet as the principal accent. The light stage carries overlapping white interface fragments and peripheral context; the dark stage gives a single product surface or short statement room to lead. A short sequence may start on either stage, but a full demonstration should exercise both.

Use solid white panel faces, near-black body text, pale gray rules and muted secondary labels. Violet marks active buttons, selected states, key words, outlines and connector paths. Reserve green for small positive-state indicators when the data needs them. Avoid rainbow decoration. Inferred working swatches: light field #F5F4FC, lavender wash #E8E5FF, dark field #080710, deep-indigo pool #19153A, main violet #6B55ED, pale glow #B2A4FF, text #15131B, muted gray #878590. These are practical visual matches, not sampled brand tokens.

**Core, observed:** Keep interface faces opaque and crisp, with rounded corners, a fine violet rim and a soft outward violet halo. On light backgrounds use a lavender shadow; on dark backgrounds the bright edge glows against the surrounding void. The result is flat UI with optical depth, not frosted glass, extruded hardware or heavy material texture. Small chips can be more rounded than full application windows. Keep glow outside text-bearing faces so the information remains clear.

A broad, low-contrast violet light pool can sit near a dark-stage corner or edge. A faint angled copy of a product surface may recede behind the foreground. These are observed defaults; do not replace negative space with dense particles, a futuristic grid or a star field.

## Organise information through depth

**Core, observed:** Establish one sharp foreground product surface or one dominant headline; push supporting interface fragments toward the edges with reduced contrast, smaller scale or blur. The light environment may feel busy, but the foreground reading path must remain obvious. Background fragments should contain recognisable domain structure, rather than being arbitrary rectangles. They may be partially cropped by the frame.

Use a full application window when a workflow needs navigation, tabs, rows and a detail area. Use a compact detached card when a single action or result is the message. Show plausible labels, data, selected states and controls; do not replace the entire application with giant marketing cards. Local UI microcopy is subordinate to the main claim and does not all need to be read during a wide shot.

Observed default layouts include a short left-hand claim paired with a right-hand card cluster, a centered application under a top caption, and a lone centered line on the dark field. Product surfaces are often approximately one-half to three-quarters of the frame width, with larger crops reserved for interaction details. These ratios are approximate suggestions. Keep essential labels and the active control within the visible frame even when the rest of the surface crops out.

**Conditional, observed:** When showing several inputs or participants feeding one result, connect small satellite chips to the dominant card with thin violet curves, and reveal those chips in sequence. The chips must name real entities for the new topic. Exact chip count, portraits and positions are variable; portraits are only appropriate for people. Curves communicate a relationship, not decorative circuitry.

## Type that reveals the claim

**Core, observed:** Set short sentence-case claims in a clean sans serif, with regular supporting words and a bolder or violet key phrase. Use one to three compact lines for a sustained claim. Preserve ordinary word spacing once the text settles. Uppercase is suitable for tiny UI section labels, not every headline. Long paragraphs, ornate editorial type and monospaced code typography are outside the default identity.

The visible source has neutral grotesk letterforms, open counters and direct, unembellished terminals. Main claims are much larger than panel microcopy. At a suggested 1080-high presentation scale, start around 54–76 px for claims, 24–36 px for panel titles and 16–24 px for compact interface text; scale for the chosen composition and reading distance. These sizes are implementation guidance, not measured source typography. An isolated bridge word may briefly fill much of the frame, then give way to ordinary-scale information; this is an optional punctuation move, not the baseline text size.

**Default, inferred font match:** Use Google font [Inter](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter) for English/Latin claims and interface text: upright 400 for supporting phrases and body, 500 for controls and compact labels, 600 for titles, and 700 for emphasis. Use the real weights of the variable family, not synthetic bold. Inter's neutral forms, open counters and firm heavier weights are a practical match, not an identification of the original font. Verified Google Fonts metadata provides upright weights 100–900 and Latin/Latin-ext, Greek, Cyrillic and Vietnamese subsets. This style's specified use and proof scope are English/Latin plus numerals. CJK typography is unspecified and requires a separately verified companion before multilingual reuse. A supplied brand mark may retain its own lettering; do not recreate a reference logo. Without a supplied mark, an Inter wordmark is an inferred, simpler fallback.

## Motion: reveal, act, resolve

**Core, observed:** Reveal a claim progressively from left to right, with brief opacity or blur differences and temporary expanded spacing that settles into a crisp, normally spaced line. Do not leave the finished line faint or widely tracked. This is a quick entrance followed by a reading hold, rather than a perpetual shimmer. Another observed option is character typing with a vertical caret for a short unresolved question. Keep such typing brief and actually reach the complete phrase.

**Core, observed:** Bring product panels forward with a smooth combination of translation, scale and a slight perspective tilt; let them settle before the important action. Camera-like moves may push into a specific field and pull back to its result. The readable face should remain dominant; avoid repeated flips, elastic bouncing or constant orbiting. Panel travel feels eased and continuous. Exact easing curves and source animation cadence are unknown; do not simulate low-frame-rate stop motion.

**Core, observed:** A visible cursor targets a meaningful control, the control responds, and the interface changes to a concrete result. Use a bold black arrow on light panels. The pointer must land on the actual control before the state change. A short press, highlight or local glow can confirm the action. Let the changed labels, rows or success indicator be legible before moving on. This causal sequence is more important than a literal mouse-click ring. Do not use a free-floating cursor with no consequence.

**Core transition, observed:** Use a violet four-point sparkle with deeply concave curved sides as an oversized moving wipe between the light and dark stages. The concave four-point silhouette must be recognizable briefly before it grows beyond the frame; a lone curved edge crossing the screen is insufficient. It grows or sweeps across enough of the frame to obscure the outgoing composition, then clears to reveal the next. It is a moving silhouette with broad curved cutouts and a soft edge glow, not a tiny decorative star, straight-edged diamond or a stationary icon under a crossfade. Rotation and direction can vary. Reuse it sparingly as punctuation between meaningful beats. A bright wash is another observed transition, but the curved sparkle is the more distinctive signature.

Timing is flexible. An observed short line resolves over roughly half a second; the large sparkle transition occupies about a second in the inspected examples. As inferred working defaults, allow roughly 0.4–0.8 s for a short claim reveal, 0.7–1.1 s for a large wipe, and at least 1–2 s of readable stillness around a result. Longer copy or denser data needs longer holds. These are not requirements to cut on a musical beat; audio synchronization was not established.

## Native moves and permitted variation

- **Dispersed fragments to one clear operation:** begin with several domain-specific fragments around a claim, then make one actionable product surface dominant. Keep visual continuity through violet and repeated panel geometry.
- **Relationship cluster:** a central record gains named satellite chips and thin curved links. Use it when multiple inputs or people contribute to a result, not for every scene.
- **Action spotlight:** crop or push toward the relevant control, show the cursor intervention, then reveal an updated state. Retain enough surrounding UI to explain what changed.
- **Typographic punctuation:** a brief large word or progressively revealed line separates feature demonstrations. Keep the complete message readable after the entrance.
- **Quiet closure:** let a small brand mark or short resolution line settle on the dark stage with ample empty space. Custom logo geometry is variable and is not part of this transferable grammar.

Subjects, data, copy, number of windows and arrangement can change. Preserve the contrast between contextual collage and focused action, the white UI surfaces, restrained violet, short weighted claims, curved sparkle transition and readable causal motion. A new topic should supply its own convincing interface behaviour; copying calendar content, people's names or a reference brand does not demonstrate transfer. Avoid generic neon spectacle, all-dark dashboards, placeholder-only interfaces, and long rapid-cut sequences with no readable outcome.
